Dispatch policy corrections and evaluation separation

The 110-entry kernel table was frozen before performance validation. It did not
change during either routing correction below.

1. Initial policy: exact M, otherwise any anchor with equal ceil(M/token_tile),
   otherwise log-nearest new-table M. The 90 kernel-search holdout points exposed
   regressions at M=65 and M=129. validation_initial/ retains all 200 first-pass
   points. These 90 points became policy regression tests.
2. Added guard: only equal-tile anchors within 8 tokens; otherwise use the original
   optimized.json dispatch. Added 40 new points at M=[23,39,95,130,191,258,510,1026].
   Cross-GPU measurements subsequently confirmed an 8% regression at M=511 for
   the direct-load short-K kernel. validation_before_shortk_guard/ and
   crosscheck_before_shortk_guard/ preserve this stage.
3. Final guard: short-K is exact-M only, preserving its M=512 benefit and avoiding
   masked-tail interpolation. M=510/511 were revalidated and cross-GPU retimed.
   The first two off-grid batches form the 130-point policy regression set.

Final independent policy holdout: M=[47,79,194,509] across all five N/K pairs,
20 points not used to select kernels or modify the final policy.

Final dataset: 110 search points + 130 policy regression points + 20 final
holdout points = 260. Changed selections were revalidated/retimed; unchanged
selections retain their existing independent timing evidence. Every entry in
the distributable package was additionally checked with another input seed.

All initial observations, including failures and regressions, remain available.
